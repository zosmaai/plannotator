## Inbox: signing in with an account

A third way to reach the Inbox from another device, after the tailnet and Cloudflare Access: a public name, one account of the Inbox's own, and a password. No Cloudflare account, no tailnet, no Access application. Off and invisible until `plannotator inbox auth on` runs, and off again with `auth off`.

It is built on [Better Auth](https://better-auth.com) (`better-auth` in `packages/server/package.json`), so what the person gets is the library's own, well-tested sign-in: email and password, hashed with its own scrypt, session cookies, session revocation on a password change or an account removal, and rate limiting. Nothing about passwords, hashing, cookies or sessions is written here.

**Files.**
- `packages/shared/inbox/auth-door.ts`: settings (config.json `inboxAuth`, `PLANNOTATOR_INBOX_AUTH`, `PLANNOTATOR_INBOX_AUTH_HOST`, `PLANNOTATOR_INBOX_AUTH_ALLOW`, `PLANNOTATOR_INBOX_AUTH_PORT`), the allowlist, `safeNextPath`, `withoutAuthCookies`, `isAuthOwnedPath`, the cookie name.
- `packages/server/inbox-auth-instance.ts`: the Better Auth instance, its sqlite file, the account calls the CLI and the door share (`list`, `set`, `remove`), `sessionUser`.
- `packages/server/inbox-auth-door.ts`: the listener, the checks, the dispatch, the registry record.
- `packages/server/inbox-auth-login.ts`: the sign-in page, the passkeys page, `/auth.css`, `/auth.js`, `/passkeys.js`.
- `packages/server/passkey-fixtures.ts`: a software WebAuthn authenticator, imported by tests only.
- `packages/server/inbox-auth-routes.ts`: `/api/inbox/sign-in*` — the card's routes, and `refuseDoorOrigin`, which turns away a request that came through the door before they run.
- `packages/inbox/components/WithAnAccount.tsx` + `packages/inbox/data/inboxAuth.ts`: the card itself, and its read (`readInboxAuth`) and writes (`postInboxAuth`, which turns a refusal into `InboxAuthRefusal`).
- `apps/hook/server/inbox-auth-command.ts`: `plannotator inbox auth add | list | remove | on | off | status`.
- `packages/inbox/components/AskTwice.tsx`: the second click that removes an address or a phone.

**Two listeners, one window.** The window keeps its loopback port and its URL, untouched: nothing about it changes, and a start with no `inboxAuth` in config.json behaves exactly as before. Signing in happens on a **second loopback listener** (`127.0.0.1:<port>`, `19501` unless `--port` says otherwise), which is the only target a tunnel or proxy points at. That port answers its own name only (check 0), so a name pointed at it by mistake, or by someone else, signs nobody in.

**The door.** Every request passes these checks in order; the first failure answers JSON `{error, code}` on `/api/*` and `/mcp`, plain text elsewhere, `no-store`:
0. `Host` is the configured name (`:443` or none): anything else, including another name pointed at this port, is `403 forbidden_host`.
1. `/mcp`, every connection route (`/api/inbox/control/*`, `/api/inbox/bridge/*`) and a launched session's permission hook (`/api/inbox/launch/<key>/permission`) are refused (`403 local_only`), signed in or not: an agent's work never comes in from the internet.
2. The sign-in page's own paths (`/login`, `/auth.css`, `/auth.js`) and Better Auth's (`/api/auth/*`) are the door's, and are answered here.
3. A session it can read, and whose address is on the allowlist: the request goes to the window, dispatched as `served(req, "tailnet")` exactly as the Cloudflare door dispatches (no `/mcp`, no connection route, no device door).
4. No session: an HTML navigation (a `sec-fetch-dest` of `document`, or an `Accept` that names `text/html` with a method that is `GET` or `HEAD`) is `303` to `/login?next=<the path>`, and anything else is `401` (`auth_required`, or `account_not_allowed` for a session whose address is off the allowlist).

Before it reaches the window, `Cf-*`, `Tailscale-*`, `X-Forwarded-*` and `Plannotator-Door*` are dropped (`scrubAuthDoorHeaders`), and the door's own session cookies are dropped too (`withoutAuthCookies`): the window has never heard of them, and its routes can never be made to read one.

**The sign-in page.** One HTML page, no framework, its own CSP (`default-src 'none'`, `connect-src 'self'`), served with the form in it so the first paint has the fields: an address, a password, the door's host name, and the `next` path as a hidden field. `next` is `safeNextPath(...)`: a path on this site only (`//host`, another origin, `/\host`, the sign-in page itself and `/api/…` all become `/`). Already signed in, a visit to `/login` is a `303` to that `next`, so a bookmark of the form never traps someone who is already in. A failed sign-in says one line — Better Auth's own code mapped to plain words (`INVALID_EMAIL_OR_PASSWORD`, `TOO_MANY_REQUESTS` and so on) — and never says whether the address exists. `/auth.js` is small and does one thing: fetch `POST /api/auth/sign-in/email`, then `location.assign(next)`; with no JavaScript the form posts to the same path and the door answers the `303` itself.

**Passkeys.** A passkey is a second way through this door, beside the password, built on Better Auth's own `@better-auth/passkey` plugin (1.7.25, the same release as `better-auth`). The door adds no ceremony code: `/auth.js` calls the plugin's `generate-authenticate-options`, then `navigator.credentials.get`, then `verify-authentication`, and the session that comes back is the same `__Secure-plannotator.session_token` a password makes, checked by the same allowlist. The relying party is the door's public name — `rpID` is `inboxAuth.host`, the origin is `settings.baseUrl` — because a passkey belongs to the name the person is on, not to the loopback address behind it. So:

- At `https://<host>` (what the tunnel serves) passkeys work in every browser that has WebAuthn.
- At any other address they do not. Browsers run WebAuthn on https only, so the page says so under the button ("Passkeys need `https://<host>`. The password works everywhere."), and a click on an insecure origin is answered with that line instead of a `TypeError` from a `navigator.credentials` that is not there. `http://127.0.0.1:<port>` is not a second way in: the `Host` check refuses anything but the configured name there (`403 forbidden_host`), so the door is only ever used at the public name.
- The naming is the whole of it: a passkey is made for `inboxAuth.host` (`rpID`), so changing that host silently stops existing passkeys being offered — the rows stay listed on `/passkeys`, and each new passkey is made for the new name.

**Managing them.** `/passkeys` is the door's own page, reachable only with a session (that is the gate: a person who has not signed in cannot reach it at all). It lists this account's passkeys by name and date, adds one from the device in front of it, and removes one. The calls behind it — `GET /api/inbox/auth/passkeys` and `POST /api/inbox/auth/passkeys/remove` — answer for the session's own account and never take an address from the request, and they never hand back a stored key: a name, a date, the door's own row id (`id`), and `deviceType`/`backedUp` for the words. The last passkey of an account with no password cannot be removed (`409 last_way_in`): a passkey is a second way in, never the only way out, and an account with a password can give its last one up because the password is still there (every account `auth add` makes has a password, so that guard is defence in depth rather than a state the product reaches). `plannotator inbox auth status` prints the page's address, because nothing else links to it.

**What a passkey does not change.** The allowlist is still the gate: a passkey sign-in for an address that is not on `inboxAuth.allow` gets a real session from Better Auth and is then refused by the door (`303 /login?error=account` for a page, `401 account_not_allowed` for a call), exactly as a password sign-in would be. `/mcp`, the connection routes and a launched session's permission hook stay closed to everyone; the door's cookies still stop at the door; adding a passkey needs a session, so nobody can enrol one onto an account they have not already got into.

**Accounts, made here.** There is no self-sign-up (`disableSignUp: true`): accounts are made at the terminal, `plannotator inbox auth add <address>`, with the password asked for twice, not echoed, at least 12 characters (`AUTH_MIN_PASSWORD_LENGTH`), and its address must already be on the allowlist to get in. `auth add` on an existing address sets a new password and ends every session it had (`AUTH_SESSION_SECONDS` is 7 days, renewed at most daily). `auth remove` ends them and deletes the account. The accounts and their sessions live in sqlite in the data folder (`<dataDir>/auth.sqlite`, WAL), and Better Auth's signing secret beside it in `<dataDir>/auth-secret` (0600, 32 random bytes, made once), so a restart keeps sessions; `PLANNOTATOR_AUTH_SECRET` overrides the file, which is what a test uses.

**The allowlist is the gate, the account is the key.** `inboxAuth.allow` names the addresses that may enter; a signed-in session whose address is not on it is refused (`account_not_allowed`, and the page says so), and `auth list` marks the account as not on the allowlist rather than hiding it. Take an address off the allowlist and it cannot get in, though its account stays for the day it is put back; `auth remove` is the other half.

**Rate limiting.** Better Auth's own: 60 requests a minute per client address, and 10 sign-in attempts per address per 5 minutes (`customRules`). The client's address comes from the tunnel in front of the door (`cf-connecting-ip`, else the first `x-forwarded-for` hop), which keeps one guesser from using up everyone's budget; with neither header, all clients share one bucket, so a door behind a proxy that forwards neither header limits everyone together, which is the safe direction.

**Config and the running Inbox.** `auth on` writes config.json (`{on, host, allow, port}`) and then asks a running Inbox to open the door now, through `POST /api/inbox/control/auth` (a connection route: this machine, the Inbox's token, no Origin), which re-reads config.json, opens, closes or moves the listener and writes the record into the registry (`inbox_auth: {on, url, port, allow, error}`), the same way the Cloudflare door does. With no Inbox running it says the door opens at the next start. `auth off` closes it and keeps the accounts.

**In Settings → Remote devices.** (`packages/inbox/components/WithAnAccount.tsx`, `packages/inbox/data/inboxAuth.ts`, under "Sign in with a Plannotator account".) The card reads the door once when the section mounts — it is opened by nothing else, because it does not change what is on the rest of Settings — and draws one of five things, said the same way on the card, in the CLI and in this guide. No `inboxAuth` in config.json: a line and a **Set up** button; the form that follows asks for the name the door answers at, the addresses that may enter, and the first account's address and password, and makes the account *before* it opens the door, because an open door nobody can enter is a broken Inbox. Then the switch (**Use your account to get in**), the address in the address bar (`https://<host>/login`) with its own **Copy** button, the accounts (never a password), and the list of addresses that may enter, each removable and asked twice (`components/AskTwice.tsx`, the same second click as removing a paired phone). **Use your account to get in** off (config.json `on: false`, listener closed): the address line goes away and the accounts and the list stay, with "Closed. The name still answers, but nobody gets in. Your accounts are kept." — the same words the CLI says. And if the door itself answers that it will not change from where it is being read, the card shows "Only the Inbox's owner changes how you get in. Change this on the computer itself or over your tailnet." and offers nothing to click, rather than buttons that would only fail. Which requests those are is the owner's and nobody else's: anyone who came in through the sign-in door, and anyone who came through Cloudflare Access, are both refused (`refuseDoorOrigin`, and the `fromCfDoor` mark the Cloudflare card already answers with). The first because a person signed in at a door must not change the accounts and the allow list of the door that let them in; the second because Access admits a whole team, and a teammate is not the owner of this Inbox.

Everything the card writes goes to `/api/inbox/sign-in*` (the rows in [`server-and-storage.md`](server-and-storage.md)), which is this computer's or the tailnet owner's, never a paired phone's or a relay's, and never through the door: the card shows its actions while the door's own read says *what* the door is, and writes only from where the door can be written. The password floor is one number in one place, `AUTH_MIN_PASSWORD_LENGTH` (`packages/server/inbox-auth-instance.ts`), which the card shows as it is (`password, 12 characters or more`) instead of saying 8 in one place and checking 12 in another.

**Not built, and why.** TOTP two-factor, password reset by email, invites with a link, passkey renaming, recovery codes and more than one door at a time: each is a next step on top of Better Auth's own plugins, and none is needed to let one person in at a public name. `auth add` is the invite, and the person at the machine is the only one who makes accounts. TOTP's shape is written up in `adr/implementation/inbox-auth.md` rather than half-built here.
